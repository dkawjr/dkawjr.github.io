Research

Visual perception and computational neuroscience

In the Coen-Cagli Laboratory at Einstein, my doctoral research examines how autistic and neurotypical adults organize natural scenes into distinct regions. Participants judge whether two locations in an image belong to the same region, allowing us to measure how spatial expectations influence perception. I combine these judgments with EEG, eye tracking, and computational modeling to study when perceptual decisions emerge and how people combine visual evidence with prior expectations. I lead the EEG analyses, including time-resolved decoding of participants’ choices, and develop reproducible workflows for preprocessing, artifact control, statistical testing, and visualization.

Perioperative physiology and clinical AI

With Joseph Scarpa, MD, PhD, and collaborators at Weill Cornell Medicine, I develop and evaluate methods using EEG, ECG, photoplethysmography, capnography, arterial pressure, clinical records, and laboratory data. Current work includes anesthetic EEG monitoring, capnogram morphology, pulse-oximetry waveform analysis, and physiological interpretation of machine-learning models.

I use participant-separated evaluation, comparison with clinical baselines, and explicit checks for data leakage and changes in performance across datasets.

NINscan and brain–body physiology

I collaborate with Julia Scarpa, MD, PhD, an Assistant Professor of Anesthesiology in the Medical Accelerator and Digital Engineering (MADE) Lab at Weill Cornell Medicine, where she works alongside Joseph Scarpa, MD, PhD. I also work with Gary Strangman, PhD, Associate Professor of Psychology in the Department of Psychiatry at Harvard Medical School. Gary directs the Neural Systems Group and serves as Managing Director of the Center for Space Medicine Research at Massachusetts General Hospital.

In this collaboration, I analyze wearable near-infrared recordings alongside cardiac, respiratory, and perioperative signals. I develop signal-quality, synchronization, and individual-level workflows for resting recordings, postural and breathing challenges, and comparisons between awake and anesthetized states.

This ongoing work concerns physiological regulation, frailty, and cognition, with interests in perioperative and space-health applications.

Research methods and software

I work in Python, MATLAB, and R, using MNE-Python, FSL, statistical modeling, and machine learning. I build reusable tools for analysis, visualization, provenance, and reproduction of saved results.
